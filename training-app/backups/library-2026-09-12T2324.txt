[Good Drills] GOOD DRILLS - How they work
======================================================================
GOOD DRILLS - HOW THEY WORK
Read once. This page is the rule for every Good page in the library.

WHAT A GOOD DRILL IS
A Good drill is not a shooting drill or a finishing drill. The shot at the end is the outcome,
not the point. The point is the mess before it.
You are training three things and only these three:
  PERCEPTION - how little time you need to see it and decide
  BALANCE - producing force from positions you should not be able to produce force from
  PACE - doing both of the above at game speed instead of workout speed
GOOD DRILLS APPLY TO SKILLS, NOT SITUATIONS. Handling, shooting, finishing - things you
execute. Off ball, ball screens and team defense are positioning and decisions, not skills, and
they belong in the reps rotation and at practice instead.

DRILLS ARE COMPOUND, NOT ONE CONCEPT AT A TIME
A good drill pays several bills in one rep. Crawl start, spin, spin back, touch the backboard,
adjust and finish on the way down is off-balance AND max height AND an air adjustment AND almost
no time to see the rim - four targets, one rep. Build them that way. A drill that trains exactly
one thing is usually a drill that could have trained three.

WHY THERE ARE NO MAKE STANDARDS ON THESE PAGES
Because the way you hit a make standard on a deliberately awkward rep is by making it less
awkward. Shorter fall, earlier gather, straighter entry, easier angle. A make count on a Good
drill quietly pays you to delete the drill.
Bag pages have make standards. 50/50 has a make standard. Good pages do not. Different job.

HOW YOU PROGRESS - THE LEVEL, NOT THE MAKES
Every Good page runs at a LEVEL. The level is how uncomfortable the setting is. You move up when
you can hold the QUALITY at the current level, not when you make more shots.

L1  Clean entry. Learn the shape of the drill.
L2  Awkward entry - fall start, crawl start, spin start, off-balance, on the move already
L3  Less time - the ball arrives late, you go before you are set, no gather you did not earn
L4  Full pace - sprint into everything, no walking between reps
L5  Late decision - the cue comes as late as you can make it come
L6  Tired - run it after conditioning or at the end of the block, not fresh
L7  Two at once - stack any two of L2 to L6

THE QUALITY TEST - this is what you are judging, every rep
Did you finish the rep balanced enough to do something else immediately after it?
If you land, stumble, and reset, the level is too high. If you never feel the edge, it is too low.
You want to be on the edge of losing it and not losing it. That is the whole training effect.

MOVING UP
Hold the quality for two sessions at a level, go up. Lose the quality twice, go back down.
Write the level on the NOW line next to the drill. There is no shame in L2 forever on the hardest drill on the page -
levels are per DRILL, not per page. Some of your drills will sit at L5 and some at L2 and that
is the system working, not the system failing.

WHAT TO FILM
Once a month, film one drill from each Good page at your current level. You cannot feel your own
balance and you cannot feel your own pace. You can see both.

[Good Drills] Good Finishing
======================================================================
GOOD FINISHING
Compound drills. No make standards except X layups. Scale by LEVEL, not by makes.
Write your level next to each drill. Move it when you own it, drop it when you do not.
Two Good Handles drills come first, before this page. Some drills here also enter through
handling - see the entries menu below.

HANDLING ENTRIES - the menu. Any drill below can be entered through one of these.
Higher levels on every ladder call for one. This is where handling in awkward situations and
sharpening moves live now - they are how you ARRIVE, not a separate workout.
  CHASE IT       throw it ahead off the wall or a hard roll, sprint it down, pick it up moving
  SCRAMBLE UP    start on the floor with the ball already bouncing, get to your feet, go
  OUT OF THE BOX two chairs a body width apart, work it low and fast, explode out
  TWO BALL DROP  two going, drop one, immediately go with the other
  OFF A COUNTER  killer cross to burnsey, in and out to spin, reverse through to euro,
                 misdirection through and behind, lowmans
THE SHARPNESS TEST: did the move take you somewhere? Alone, a move that ends where it started
was decoration. Ground covered is the measure.

YOUR FOUR TARGETS AND WHERE THEY GET HIT
                              glass height | perception | air adjust | spots+hands
Crawl Spin Touch Finish            core         core         core         some
Fall Start Menu                    some         some         some         core
Crawl Into Touch                   -            core         some         some
Loose Ball Finish                  -            core         some         core
Every session runs 2 or 3 of these. All four targets get hit either way.

1. CRAWL SPIN TOUCH FINISH                                    NOW: L___
Crawl start, spin, spin back, jump and touch as high as you can, adjust in the air, finish on
the way down.
Off balance from the spins, max height on the glass, an adjustment mid-air, and almost no time to
see the rim because you are already coming down. Four targets, one rep.
  L1  Crawl start, one spin, touch the NET, finish. Strong hand.
  L2  Touch the rim instead of the net
  L3  Touch the backboard
  L4  Add the spin back
  L5  Both hands
  L6  Get up later off the crawl - less time before you have to go
  L7  Finish is called AT the touch, not before it
  L8  Off arm pinned to your side - no balance arm
  L9  Land on one foot, hold it two seconds
  L10 Enter it off a handling entry
  Start at whatever height you can actually reach. Touching the net cleanly beats missing the
  backboard. The touch is the hardest part of this drill and there is no shame in climbing it.

2. FALL START MENU                                            NOW: L___
3/4 court fall start, through through through, into a finish off the menu.
Touch backboard | Reverse | Free throw extension | Same foot same hand high | Pinoy slow |
Free throw float | 1 foot change feet and hands
  L1  Walk-in speed, fixed finish, strong hand
  L2  Full speed
  L3  Both hands, every finish
  L4  Finish called at the free throw line
  L5  Finish called at the block
  L6  Full court instead of 3/4
  L7  Off arm pinned to your side
  L8  Enter it off a handling entry
  L9  Land on one, hold

3. CRAWL INTO TOUCH                                           NOW: L___
Crawl start into a fade or a float. Soft hands off a bad position.
  L1  Crawl into fade, strong hand
  L2  Crawl into float
  L3  Both hands on both
  L4  Get up later
  L5  Which one is called as you rise
  L6  Off one foot
  L7  Off hand holding a second ball
  L8  Enter it off a handling entry

4. LOOSE BALL FINISH                                          NOW: L___
Roll it out or off the wall, chase it down, pick it up, finish from wherever you got it.
  L1  Slow roll, strong hand, any finish
  L2  Faster roll
  L3  Roll it to your weak side
  L4  Pick it up going backwards
  L5  Bad bounce off the wall instead of a roll
  L6  Finish called as you pick it up
  L7  From a dive to your feet
  L8  Off a counter into the finish - burnsey, spin layup, euro

X LAYUPS - 2/2 or it doesnt count. No level, no scaling.
Same foot same hand | Euro | Wide down the lane | Pinoy
Your clean reference - the version with nothing wrong with it, so you know the finish itself is
sound before you go make it hard.

HOW TO SCALE
Move up when you can run the whole rep without a reset - land balanced enough to go again
immediately. Drop a level when you cannot, and drop it without arguing.
Levels are per DRILL. Sitting at L2 on one and L6 on another is the system working.
When a level moves, overwrite the NOW line next to that drill. That is the only tracking this needs.

GOOD SHOOTING
Compound drills. No make standards. Scale by LEVEL. Write your level next to each drill.
Two Good Handles drills come first, before this page. Some drills here also enter through

HANDLING ENTRIES - the menu. Any drill below can be entered through one of these.
Higher levels on every ladder call for one. This is where handling in awkward situations and
sharpening moves live now - they are how you ARRIVE, not a separate workout.
  CHASE IT       throw it ahead off the wall or a hard roll, sprint it down, pick it up moving
  SCRAMBLE UP    start on the floor with the ball already bouncing, get to your feet, go
  OUT OF THE BOX two chairs a body width apart, work it low and fast, explode out
  TWO BALL DROP  two going, drop one, immediately go with the other
  OFF A COUNTER  killer cross to burnsey, in and out to spin, reverse through to euro,
                 misdirection through and behind, lowmans
THE SHARPNESS TEST: did the move take you somewhere? Alone, a move that ends where it started
was decoration. Ground covered is the measure.

YOUR FIVE TARGETS AND WHERE THEY GET HIT
                          balance | release speed | perception | deeper | awkward pickup
Spin Spin Back Pull Up      core        some          some        opt         -
Roll And Pick Up            core         some         core        opt        core
Lose The Rim                some        core          core        opt         -
Sprint Into It              core        some          some        opt         some
Skater Stepback             core        some           -          opt         -
Deep Work                    -           -             -         core         -
Two or three drills a session. Deep Work runs short and on its own - see why below.

1. SPIN SPIN BACK PULL UP                                     NOW: L___
Spin start, spin back, straight into a 1-dribble pull up. You leave the spin still rotating, have
to kill the rotation, and the rim was behind you a half second ago.
  L1  One spin, 1 dribble pull up, midrange
  L2  Add the spin back
  L3  From three
  L4  Both directions
  L5  Fadeaway instead of the pull up
  L6  The shot is called as you come out of the second spin
  L7  One count - pickup to release inside a second
  L8  Enter it off a handling entry - chase it, scramble up, or out of the box
  L9  Run it tired, end of the block

2. ROLL AND PICK UP                                           NOW: L___
Roll it out, chase it down, pick it up, shoot from wherever you got it. Worst starting position
you own, and you never get to pre-set your feet for a spot you chose.
  L1  Slow roll, strong side, midrange
  L2  Faster roll
  L3  Roll it to your weak side
  L4  Pick it up moving backwards
  L5  Bad bounce off the wall instead of a roll
  L6  Shoot from exactly where it stopped - no stepping into a spot you like
  L7  One count from the pickup
  L8  From three
  L9  Enter it off a handling entry

3. LOSE THE RIM                                               NOW: L___
High bounce at half, back pedal, 180, no dip. You lose the rim and have to re-find it late,
and the no-dip means you cannot buy time on the way up.
  L1  Back pedal, 180, normal gather
  L2  No dip
  L3  Back pedal further
  L4  Turn later
  L5  Self-toss off the backboard behind you - you never see it until you turn
  L6  Call a number off the wall before you release
  L7  One count
  L8  From three, then a step deeper
  L9  Enter it off a handling entry

4. SPRINT INTO IT                                             NOW: L___
High bounce, touch the paint, sprint into a curl, shoot on arrival with your feet still going.
  L1  Jog into the curl
  L2  Full sprint
  L3  Release before the feet fully set
  L4  Curl then relocate - two moves before the shot
  L5  The spot is called while you are sprinting
  L6  One count
  L7  From three
  L8  Enter it off a handling entry
  L9  After conditioning

5. SKATER STEPBACK                                            NOW: L___
Force production off a leg already travelling sideways.
  L1  Skater stepback, midrange
  L2  From three
  L3  Wider skater
  L4  Both ways
  L5  Skater into a stepback the OTHER way
  L6  One count
  L7  Off a live pickup
  L8  Enter it off a handling entry

DEEP WORK - short, on its own, form first                     NOW: L___
This one does not compound. Range needs a clean release and stacking constraints on it teaches
you to heave. Keep it to a few minutes at the end.
  L1  One-hand form shots at the arc, each hand
  L2  Two feet behind the arc, normal mechanics
  L3  Four feet behind
  L4  From the logo
  THE RULE: range comes from your legs and a cleaner release, never from throwing harder. If a
  shot needs an extra dip, a hitch, or a heave, you are too deep - come back a step. Add distance
  only when the release looks identical to your free throw.

HOW TO SCALE
Move up when you can run the rep without a reset. Drop a level when you cannot, without arguing.
Levels are per drill. When one moves, overwrite the NOW line next to that drill.

[Good Drills] Good Handling
======================================================================
GOOD HANDLING
Compound drills. No make standards. Scale by LEVEL.
TWO OF THESE START EVERY MORNING, after Vitamins and before Good Shooting or Good Finishing.
Rotate which two. Over a week all five get run.
Every rep on this page ends in a MOVE THAT GOES SOMEWHERE, at speed, out of a position you did
not want to be in. If a rep ends in a dribble, it did not count.

YOUR THREE TARGETS AND WHERE THEY GET HIT
                        speed | awkward | sharpening
Chase And Counter        core     core       core
Scramble To Attack       core     core       some
Tight To Open            core     core       core
Two Ball Breakout        core     some       some
Two or three a session.

THE MOVE MENU - every drill below finishes into one of these
Killer cross to burnsey | In and out to spin layup | Reverse through to euro |
Misdirection through and behind | Lowmans
The drills are the delivery. These are the content. Rotate which one you feed in.

1. CHASE AND COUNTER                                          NOW: L___
Throw it ahead, sprint it down, pick it up on the move, straight into a move, finish.
You never get a clean pickup and you never get to set your feet before the move.
  L1  Roll it ahead, jog, pick up, one move, finish
  L2  Full sprint
  L3  Pick it up with the weak hand
  L4  Pick it up moving AWAY from the rim, turn into the move
  L5  Bad bounce off the wall instead of a roll
  L6  The move is called as you pick it up
  L7  Chairs where you pick up, so the move has to be tight
  L8  Tired, end of the block

2. SCRAMBLE TO ATTACK                                         NOW: L___
Start on the floor with the ball already bouncing. Get up, go, move, finish.
The ball is never dead. If you have to pick it up off the floor to stand, the rep is over.
  L1  Kneeling, get up, one move, finish
  L2  Seated
  L3  Prone, dive to your feet
  L4  Full speed out of it
  L5  The move is called as you rise
  L6  Weak hand only the whole way
  L7  Chair to beat immediately out of the stand-up

3. TIGHT TO OPEN                                              NOW: L___
Two chairs a body width apart. Work the ball inside the box, low and fast, then explode out
into a move that covers ground.
The box trains the handle. The exit trains the move. It is one rep, not two.
  L1  Series in the box, then a move out
  L2  Narrower box
  L3  Knee height the whole time inside
  L4  Timed inside - 15s of work before the exit
  L5  Exit blind - eyes on a target across the gym, not on the box
  L6  Eyes on a target across the gym the whole time
  L7  Exit to your weak side every rep
  L8  Exit side called late

4. TWO BALL BREAKOUT                                          NOW: L___
Two balls going. Drop one on your own cue, immediately into a move at speed with the other.
Your hands are busy, so the move has to come out without you setting it up.
  L1  Stationary, drop one, one move
  L2  Opposite rhythm before the drop
  L3  On the move before the drop
  L4  Which ball you keep is called late
  L5  Full sprint out of it into the finish

HOW TO SCALE
Move up when you can run the rep without losing the ball AND the move still covers ground.
If you never nearly lose it, the level is too low.
THE TEST FOR SHARPNESS: did the move actually take you somewhere? Alone, with nobody to beat, a
move that ends where it started was decoration. Ground covered is the whole measure.
When a level moves, overwrite the NOW line next to that drill.

[Good Drills] Passing
======================================================================
PASSING
10-15 min, four times a week - Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday. Afternoon.
Two things only: can you hit a target, and do you know which one.
Not a Good drill, but the same idea - you should be off balance and moving fast when the ball
leaves your hand. A pass thrown from a good position is not the pass you need to practice.

THE TARGETS - three, on a wall
LEFT | MIDDLE | RIGHT
Tape or chalk. Keep them SMALL - a target you can hit from anywhere is not a target.
The wall is the point: it rebounds instantly, it needs no setup, and it is always available.

THE SHAPE OF EVERY REP
1. An entry that makes you fast, off balance, or both
2. Split Second calls the target while you are already moving
3. Hit it, with the pass the round is on

ENTRIES - pick one per round, rotate them
Live dribble at speed
Off a jump stop
Off the wrong foot
Out of a spin
Drifting or falling away from the wall
Off a loose-ball pickup

PASSES - the ROUND names the pass, the CALL names the target
Pocket | Hook | One-hand push | Skip | Drop-off | Wrap | Lob
Change the pass every round so the same three targets get hit six different ways across the week.

THE APP
Split Second, Passing section. Three calls - Left, Middle, Right - and all three are baked audio,
so they come through a Bluetooth speaker like the beeps do.
It asks how many you hit at the end of the set. That number is the whole record.

LEVEL                                                        NOW: L___
  L1  Stationary, target known before you start
  L2  Off a live dribble, target known
  L3  Split Second calls it - the call lands as you gather
  L4  Full speed
  L5  Off-balance entries only
  L6  Releasing as you move AWAY from the wall
  L7  Weak hand
  L8  Call names the target AND the pass
  L9  Two stacked - off balance at full speed, weak hand

THE STANDARD
Hit the target. A pass that misses it is a turnover with extra steps.
Eyes to the target LAST. Stare it down the whole way and the rep does not count.
Every pass off a live dribble or a gather. Never from a standstill above L1.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT NEED
Live reps. You pass more than you shoot at practice and so does everyone - the live half takes
care of itself. This block exists for accuracy under stress and for the decision, nothing else.

[Bag shooting] READS - The Daily Block
======================================================================
READS - THE DAILY BLOCK
Every action, every read, every day. Afternoon.
Good drills make the skill hard. Reps make it automatic. Neither one makes you decide, because
every rep in both is a move you picked before you started. This is the block where you do not
know the answer when you begin the rep.

HOW IT RUNS
Run every section, every day. You set the reps per section - 20 to 30 is the working range, and
some sections deserve more than others. Split Second calls it, you execute.
The setup changes between sections - cone as the screener, chair at the slot, spot beyond the arc.
Reset it and keep moving. The changeover is not rest.

THE SCORE
Makes out of the reps you set. One number per section, entered in the app the moment the set ends.
A rep is a make when you did what the call said AND finished it.
Right read, missed finish is not a make. Wrong read that happens to go in is not a make either.

HOW IT GETS HARDER - the call comes later, the shot does not get harder
                                                             NOW: L___
L1  Three or four calls live. The call lands during the countdown.
L2  The full call set live. More options to choose between is a harder read at the same reps.
L3  The call lands late - at the screen, at the catch, at the gather.
L4  Execute window tightened. 4s, then 3s, then 2s.
L5  NOT BUILT YET - two actions chained, the second call coming off the first. This needs app
    work and is on the list. Do not wait on it to run L1 through L4.

MOVING UP
Beat your number twice at a level, then move up. The app flags it when you have.
Levels are per SECTION, not per page. Ice sitting at L4 while Closeout sits at L2 is the system
working, not the system failing.

THE SECTIONS - the whole list runs, every day
Ball screen coverages   Drop | Switch-Hedge | Ice | DHO
Everything else         Closeout | Float and Drop Reads | Fade
The skill sections      Finishing | Catch and Shoot
HANDLING IS GONE FROM THE APP, not just off the list - the section and its audio were removed on
8/31. Four of its seven calls were warmup drill names - Burnsey, Lowman, Reverse through,
Misdirection - which got read as game moves and you corrected. It comes back when you dictate the
moves you would actually use to beat somebody off the dribble.

WHY THE CALL IS REALISTIC
You can see the coverage - the defense calls it out loud as you come into the screen. Knowing it
before the rep is not a cheat, it is the game. What the app is standing in for is the part you
cannot know: what the defenders actually do inside the coverage, since they cannot plug every hole.

DEFENSE READS
The defense sections run in the defense block, not here. See the Defense page in Court movement.

[Bag shooting] ON THE MOVE - The Shooting Block
======================================================================
ON THE MOVE - THE SHOOTING BLOCK
Eight ways you arrive. Six shots off every one of them.
This absorbed Bag Shooting on 8/31 - its back pedal, its deep slides and its shot fake pull-up
moved here, and everything else on that page was already a duplicate of this one.

THE EIGHT MOVEMENTS - two fixed halves, alternate them
HALF A - off a screen
  Fade          off the screen
  Curl          off the screen
  Zip up        off an elevator or a pin
  Deep slides   off an off-ball screen
HALF B - no screen, you get there on your own
  Drift         to the corner
  Lift          to the wing
  Transition    full sprint
  Back pedal    lose the rim and re-find it late

THE SIX SHOTS - every movement runs all six
1  Catch and shoot
2  No dip
3  Jab, step back, shoot
4  Shot fake, sidestep, shoot
5  Shot fake, pull up
6  Jab, shoot

WHY IT IS IN HALVES
Eight movements times six shots is forty eight combinations, and at two attempts each that is
ninety six shots standing in one block. Half is forty eight, which is a block you will actually
finish instead of abandoning at combination thirty.
Each half carries its OWN bar, so your number is always compared against the same work. Alternate
them and both halves get run across the week.

HOW IT RUNS
One half a session. Four movements, six shots, two attempts each. Forty eight shots.
Two passes, not one block - take your two, go to the next combination, come back around for the
second pass. Same rule as every reps page.
Vary the rep inside the combination: which side, how hard you come off, exactly where you catch
it. The combination is fixed. The rep is not.

FIX YOUR DISTANCE - write it in and do not move it
The number only means something if the shot is the same shot every time. 34/48 taken a step inside
the arc and 34/48 from three are not the same day, the same way corners and the top are not the
same fifty on 50/50.

[Group workouts] NERVOUS SYSTEM - Hot and Cold
======================================================================
NERVOUS SYSTEM - HOT AND COLD
The mental side. Not reads, not IQ. The piece that decides whether the skills you built show up.
Written after the first practice with the new team: nervous walking in, mistakes compounding,
playing to not mess up, body gone weak. Hot first, then cold. This page is the tool for both.
Nothing here is scored, levelled or laddered. Confidence is not a drill. It is what evidence
leaves behind, and the only job of this page is to keep you doing things that produce evidence.

THE LOOP - why it went the way it went
Nerves put the body in a threat state. Under threat the body protects, it does not perform.
That is the weakness you felt.
With nothing specific to hunt, "don't mess up" filled the empty slot.
Avoidance goals make you slow, because every option gets risk-checked before you act.
Slow is what produces the mistakes. Each one confirms the threat. Round it goes.
The fix is not feeling more confident. It is: bring the body down a notch, fill the slot, and
cut the loop after every mistake so nothing compounds. Three tools, one each.

THE TWO BREATHS - opposite jobs, do not mix them up
DOWN  Two inhales through the nose - a full one, then a short top-up on top of it - then one long
      slow exhale through the mouth until you are empty. One of these drops arousal faster than
      anything else you can do on purpose. In a practice or a game, one to three. Five minutes of
      it is the morning practice.
UP    Short sharp inhales through the nose, quick exhale, and MOVE - sprint, jump, slap the floor.
      For when you have gone flat. It is the opposite tool.

DAILY - trains the reflex so it is there when you need it. No new time.
MORNING  Your "Meditate 5 minutes" slot. Five minutes of DOWN breaths, one after another, sitting
         up, eyes closed. That is the whole practice. You are grooving the exact breath you will
         use walking into the gym, so it works without thinking when your hands are shaking.
NIGHT    Your "Slow Breathe" line. In through the nose 4 seconds, out through the nose 6, five
         minutes, lying down. Longer out than in - that is the only rule.
GYM      The reset below runs after every miss in 50/50 and every wrong read in Reads. Dozens of
         free reps a day of the thing that stops mistakes compounding, in a place where a miss
         costs nothing.

BEFORE - walking in
The target is not calm. Calm is flat and flat gets beat. The target is ready without threat:
same heart rate, no danger in it.
1. Three DOWN breaths on the walk in. Takes the top off. Does not take you to zero.
2. Say the intention out loud. ONE, off the Practice Intentions page, chosen before you left the
   room. Now the slot is full and "don't mess up" has nowhere to sit.
3. First possession is an effort job - sprint the floor, hit somebody on a screen, talk. Something
   you cannot fail. You are in the practice before the ball has touched your hands.
Nerves and excitement are the same body. The only difference is the label you put on it. When you
feel it: "that is the body getting ready". Never "calm down" - it does not work, and it tells
you something is wrong.

BETWEEN - the reset, after every mistake, 3 seconds
Mistakes are not the problem. Carrying one into the next possession is.
The reset is physical so it does not need a thought you might not have in you:
  1. One physical cue. Wipe your hands on your shorts, or pull the jersey. Same one every time.
  2. One DOWN breath.
  3. Eyes up to the next thing - the ball, or your man's chest. Find your job.
Done. You do not review the mistake. You do not get to review it until film.
Same reset after a good play. A good play you are still thinking about is the next mistake.
This is what you groove in the gym. By the time it matters it is not a decision, it is a habit.

GONE COLD - the opposite of everything above
You will know it: passing up open shots, body heavy, hoping the play goes somewhere else. That is
the freeze branch. Slow breathing sinks you further. Do not use it here.
1. UP breaths. Two or three sharp ones, and move - sprint to your spot on the next dead ball.
2. Eyes OUT. At the rim, at his chest, at the ball. Not inward. Cold lives inside your head.
3. Voice. Call every screen, every switch, every get-back, loud. Talking is a job you cannot
   fail and it drags your attention back onto the game.
4. One effort goal for the next three possessions that skill cannot ruin: sprint both ways, box
   out every shot, get a deflection. Effort still works when skill is gone, and every one you
   finish is a piece of evidence. Evidence is the only thing confidence is made of.
An open shot is still shot. Hesitating on it is the symptom, not a fix. Shoot it and reset.

WHAT THIS IS NOT
Not a confidence ladder. Not a mood score. Not a page you level up. The only measure is whether
the tools got used, and that is yes or no.
LAST PRACTICE: walked in with the slot full? ___   reset after mistakes? ___   went cold? ___

WHEN IT IS BIGGER THAN A PAGE
If it gets to where it got - the panic, the body shutting down - and it happens again, that is a
recurring thing worth taking to somebody. CWRU counselling is free and a sports psych is standard
equipment at the level you are chasing. It is a tool, same as a lift.

TRANSITION OFFENSE
First look is up the floor on every rebound and every make, before the ball goes down. Count the possessions I looked first.

Paint first, man second. Get to the paint, then find who I have.
I take the most dangerous man, not my man, until we are matched up.
I talk on every get back. Call the ball, call who I have, out loud, every possession.
My first step after my own shot is back. Count the possessions I lost that step.

Three options on the catch and I decide on the catch, not before it. Shoot it. Attack the high shoulder. One more, and relocate.

BALL SCREEN, THE LATE READ
I do not decide before I leave. I read the coverage after the first dribble, not at the point of the screen. If I know the answer before I come off it, that was a bag rep, not a live one.
Hedge and blitz are the two coverages I have never repped anywhere. Those are the ones to go looking for at practice.

[Court movement] Movement - Drive Position
======================================================================
COURT MOVEMENT - DRIVE POSITION
Morning block, about 10 minutes, before Good Drills. No ball. This block builds the positions your ball work spends.
Rotation: A, then B, then C, then back to A. Check the LAST RUN line. First time through, today is A.
LAST RUN: ___ (A, B or C)   date ___

THE STANDARD - every rep is judged on this
Low means LOW IN STRIDE, not low in a stance. The shape is a lunge you can move out of: front shin angled forward over the foot, back leg long, chest tall, hips underneath you and not behind you.
If your chest drops to get low, you did not get low, you fell forward.
If the front heel lifts, you have run out of ankle. That is Friday and Saturday's job to fix, not something to force here.

DAILY PRIMER - every day, no rest - 3 min
Deep Split Stance Hold - 2x20s ea - front foot flat, back knee low, chest tall
Low Stride Walk - 2x10 strides - hold the bottom of every stride 2s before the next
Wall Drive Hold - 2x15s ea - hands on the wall, deep drive stride, front heel down, push the floor away
Ankle Rock in Stride - 2x8 ea - front knee travels out over the toe, heel stays down

BLOCK A - DECELERATION IN THE DRIVE - rest 45s - 7 min
Go, stop, go. Every stop is held a full 2 seconds before the next thing happens.
3-Step Drive to 2-Step Stop - 4x ea side - land in the stride position, not stood up
3-Step Drive to 1-Step Stop - 4x ea side - only once the 2-step is silent and motionless
Stop and Re-Accelerate Same Direction - 3x ea side
Stop and Re-Accelerate Opposite Direction - 3x ea side
Stop on the Inside Foot - 3x ea side
Stop on the Outside Foot - 3x ea side
If a stop needs a hop, a stutter, or a second foot to catch you, it did not count. Shorten the run-in until it does.

BLOCK B - DISSOCIATION - rest 45s - 7 min
Hips turn. Chest stays quiet.
Arc Run, Chest Square to the Rim - 4x ea way
Crossover Step to Re-Plant - 4x8 ea side
Drop Step and Open Hip Run - 4x ea side
Snake Run Through 4 Cones, Shoulders Square - 4x ea way
Zig-Zag Drive Stride, Torso Still - 3x ea way
The test: could someone watching only your chest tell which way you are about to go? If your shoulders announce it, you are turning like one piece.

BLOCK C - OFF AXIS AND RHYTHM - rest 45s - 7 min
Single-Leg Landing from an Angle - 3x3 ea - land, stick 2s, second foot never touches
Off-Balance Recovery Step - 3x5 ea - fall into the stride, catch it, hold it
Half Speed to Full Speed to Stop - 4x ea side
Full Speed to Half Speed to Full Speed - 4x ea side
Stutter to Burst - 4x ea side
Rhythm is not speed. The rep only counts if the change of pace is obvious from across the gym.

PROGRESSION
Overwrite the LAST RUN line every day. Only ever write a day you actually did.
Primer: add 5s to the holds when the last rep looks like the first. Cap 30s, then go deeper instead of longer.
Block A: progress by SHORTENING the stop, never by lengthening the run-in. Two clean sessions at the 2-step, then move to the 1-step. If the 1-step gets noisy, go back.
Block B: progress by speed, not reps. Same drills faster, same quiet torso.
Block C: progress by making the landing harder - steeper angle, less warning, eyes up.
MONTHLY BENCHMARKS - first session of the month, write the numbers here, move last month's across first:
Lane agility - time it ___ s   (last month ___ s)
5-10-5 shuttle - time it both directions ___ s / ___ s   (last month ___ s / ___ s)
Steps to stop from a full-court sprint - count them ___   (last month ___)

[Court movement] Defense
======================================================================
DEFENSE
25-30 min, afternoon, after the bag work. No makes on this page - nothing here is scored on
outcome. Scale by LEVEL, same as the Good pages.
Two halves: this page is the STRESS half. The reads half is Split Second.

WHAT ACTUALLY SEPARATES A D1 DEFENDER - everything below trains one of these five
  BRAKING       stopping from a full sprint in one step, on one leg, under control
  STAYING LOW   holding your height THROUGH a direction change. Everyone rises to change
                direction. Rising is what costs you the step.
  DISSOCIATION  opening the hip and running without turning your shoulders out of the play
  ALL WAYS      producing force forward, back, lateral, diagonal - and the transitions between
                them, which are the hard part
  RE-ACCELERATION  making force again from a position you should not be able to make force from

THE STANDARD - every rep, every drill
Hip height. If your hips come up, the rep is over - reset, do not finish it ugly.
Reps with high hips teach you to play with high hips.

1. CLOSEOUT STOP                                              NOW: L___
Sprint at the target, stop on a dime, hold it, then live with what comes next.
Braking is the whole drill. A closeout you cannot stop is not a closeout, it is a foul or a blow-by.
  L1  Close from 10 ft, stop on two feet, hold 2s
  L2  From the arc, full closeout distance
  L3  Full sprint from half court
  L4  Stop on ONE foot and hold
  L5  Stop, then immediately contain - three slides, no rise
  L6  Direction called late, as you arrive
  L7  Arrive on a bad angle, off balance, still stop
  L8  Run it after conditioning

2. STAY LOW THROUGH THE ACTION                                NOW: L___
Four chairs as screens and bodies. Navigate the whole path without your hips rising once.
Judged on height, never on time. Put a marker at your stance height and check yourself against it.
  L1  Walk the path low
  L2  Game speed
  L3  Eyes up on a fixed point - navigate the path by feel
  L4  Tighter path, less room to get skinny
  L5  A direction change at every chair
  L6  Hold the height standard the entire way, no exceptions
  L7  Tired

3. ALL WAYS                                                   NOW: L___
Slide, sprint, backpedal, open-hip run, re-plant. The modes are easy. The TRANSITIONS between
them are the skill and they are where you actually get beat.
  L1  Four modes in a fixed order
  L2  Random order
  L3  Called late, while you are already moving
  L4  Full speed
  L5  Seamless - no stutter step between modes, ever
  L6  A chair to navigate mid-sequence
  L7  Tired

4. BEATEN AND BACK                                            NOW: L___
Start already beaten. Re-accelerate, take the angle to the SPOT and not to the man, get back
in front. Getting beat is not the problem. Staying beat is.
  L1  Start at his hip
  L2  Start a full step beaten
  L3  Start turned the wrong way
  L4  Start with your back to the ball - find it, then go
  L5  Recovery angle called late
  L6  From a dead stop, no run-up to build speed
  L7  Start further beaten every session. That is the progression on this one.

HOW TO SCALE
Two or three drills a session. Move up when the hips hold at the current level. Drop a level the
moment they do not. Levels are per drill.
When a level moves, overwrite the NOW line next to that drill.

THE READS HALF - SPLIT SECOND
This page makes you able to move. It does not make you certain. Certainty is the reads half and
it needs its own Split Second sections - guarding the ball screen, closing out, off-ball.
Those do not exist yet. Dictate what the calls should be and they go in.

WHAT STILL ONLY PRACTICE CAN GIVE YOU
Help, rotations, low man, digging, when to leave your man. None of it reps alone.

[Court movement] DEFENSE READS - Split Second
======================================================================
DEFENSE READS - SPLIT SECOND SECTIONS

The reads half of Defense. Five sections, thirty calls, all side:defense.
Add them in Split Second under custom drills, or hand this page to the session that built the app.
The full JSON is saved at training-app/split-second-defense.json.

HOW THEY WORK - same as your offensive sections
The SECTION is the situation you already know you are in. The CALL is what the offense shows you
in the moment. You start moving on the countdown and find out what happened as the call lands.


CLOSEOUT D
  Close out, stop, and live with whatever he does as you arrive.
  SETUP: Chair on the wing as the shooter. Cone on his baseline side and one on his middle side marking the two drive lanes. Start in help at the nail. Close out during the countdown - the call hits as you arrive.
  Shot - High hand, contest, do not leave your feet. Then box out.   [He rises into it.]
  Baseline - Beat him to the baseline spot, wall up, force the pickup.   [He rips baseline.]
  Middle - Slide middle, force him back into help.   [He attacks your top shoulder.]
  Pump - Stay down, hands up, feet on the floor.   [Shot fake.]
  Rip - Drop step, contain, no reach.   [He rips through low and goes.]
  Reset - He gives it up. Jump to the ball, regain your gap.   [Pass back out.]

ON BALL
  Contain. Beat him to the spot, never to the man.
  SETUP: Two cones marking the lanes he can attack. Start in stance a mirror-distance off a third cone as the handler. The call hits as he goes.
  Left - Beat him to the spot, hip to hip, no rise.
  Right - Beat him to the spot, hip to hip, no rise.
  Cross - He crosses back. Change direction without your hips coming up.
  Hesi - Do not bite. Stay down, hold the gap.   [He sells the stop.]
  Snatch - He snatches back for the shot. Close the space, high hand.
  Blow by - You are beat. Open the hip, run, re-plant in front.   [He has your shoulder.]

BALL SCREEN D
  Your coverage is set before the rep. The call is what he does with the screen.
  SETUP: Cone as the screener at the slot, second cone as the handler. Pick your coverage before each round - over, under or ice - and say it out loud. Come into the screen during the countdown.
  Come off - Fight over, get skinny, re-attach at his hip before he turns the corner.
  Reject - He rejects it. Beat him back the other way - there is no screen to fight.   [He goes away from it.]
  Snake - He snakes back middle. Cross over the top and re-attach on the new side.
  Split - Do not get split. Take the middle away, force him to one side.   [He splits the two of you.]
  Pull up - He pulls up behind it. Close the gap, high hand, do not foul.
  Re-screen - He comes back to it. Reset the coverage, do not trail.

OFF BALL MOVEMENT - three drills, movement and reaction only
  This is not a tactics section. Tagging, denying and stunting are not solo work and are cut.
  What is trained here is the sudden reactive change of direction that sits underneath all of it.

  RECOVER AND SLIDE - runs today as a normal section
  Slide one way, touch the floor, sprint back to your spot, and go the way the call says.
  Left / Right / Left back / Right back. The back calls are open the hip, run, re-plant in front.
  Your legs are already committed elsewhere when the call lands. That is the drill.

  FLIP AND GO - needs the GO CUE mode, not built yet
  Slide in help. At an unpredictable moment a beep fires. Flip your hips, sprint, touch the block
  or the backboard. No call, no read - the only information is when.

  CHASE - needs the GO CUE PLUS STOPWATCH mode, not built yet
  Cone as the off-ball screen. Set up for a curl or a flare before the rep - you have to know
  which to build the drill. On the beep, chase it, get low, take the tightest line you can, tap to stop.
  You are racing your own best time. No levels on this one.

TRANSITION D
  Sprint back and know who you have before you arrive.
  SETUP: Start at the offensive block. Two markers at the far end - one wide, one at the rim. Sprint back during the countdown. The call is what you have to take.
  Ball - Pick up the ball. Nobody else is going to.
  Rim - Protect the rim first. Build the wall, take the kick later.
  Deepest - Take the deepest man, then match on the next pass.
  Match - Everyone is back. Find your man and match.
  Trailer - The trailer is the threat. Meet him above the break.

WHERE THESE CAME FROM
Closeout D is your own idea - close out then decide which way to slide.
Off Ball is the biggest gap in your whole program. Gap position, stunt and recover, tagging the
roller and low-man responsibility are the off-ball habits you have never repped once, and they
are the reason freshmen sit.
Transition D picks up the GET BACK block that was orphaned when the transition page came off the
rotation - sprinting back is easy, knowing who you have before you arrive is the skill.

WHAT NO SECTION CAN GIVE YOU
Real help rotations need four other people moving. These build the habit and the vocabulary.
The timing is a Practice Intention.

[Court movement] RECORDING - Does It Sell
======================================================================
RECORDING - DOES IT SELL
Four days a week - Sunday, Monday, Friday, Saturday. 10-15 min, at the END of the workout.
Never cold, never first. You film the move warm, when your handle and your body feel right and
you are shooting it well, because that is the version you actually own. A cold rep tells you
about the cold, not about the move.

WHAT YOU ARE JUDGING - not makes
A move is a lie you tell a defender, so the only question that matters is whether he bites.
Every clip gets one of three verdicts:
  SELLS          there is a frame in it where a defender has to commit wrong
  DOESN'T SELL   nothing in it would have moved him
  CAN'T TELL     almost always means the rep was too slow, or the camera was in the wrong place

WHERE THE CAMERA GOES - this is the whole trick
Film Protocol films you from the SIDE, because it is measuring your mechanics.
This is measuring deception, so the camera stands where the DEFENDER stands - in front of you,
about his height, looking at you. A move shot from the side cannot be judged on whether it sells,
because that is not the angle anybody is being lied to from.
Everything else follows Film Protocol: tripod or propped, never handheld, slow motion on, tape a
mark on the floor for the camera and one for your start, same height every time.

HOW MUCH
Three or four moves a session, three reps each. About a dozen clips.
Few enough that you will actually watch them back. Twenty moves filmed is twenty moves never
reviewed, and unreviewed footage is just storage.

THE COMPARISON
Keep one BEST REP clip per move. That is the reference - yours, not somebody else's film. The
version where it looked how you want it to look. Beat it and it gets replaced.
Watch the new reps against it. Same angle every time, so the comparison is real.

WHAT COMES OUT OF IT
The moves that DIDN'T SELL are the moves you work next session. That is the entire loop.
Write the date, the move, and the verdict. Over weeks moves migrate from doesn't sell to sells,
and that migration is the record.

[Court movement] Film Protocol
======================================================================
FILM PROTOCOL
The capture standard. Follow it exactly or a month-to-month comparison measures your camera
angle instead of measuring you.
This page is the hook the movement analysis plugs into once it is built.

WHEN
First session of every month. Same week, same time of day, BEFORE the session - fresh, never fatigued.

CAMERA
Phone on a tripod or propped against something. Never handheld.
Slow motion on. 120fps minimum, 240fps if your phone offers it.
Tape a mark on the floor for the camera and leave the tape there. Tape your starting spot too.
Same camera height every time. Hip height is the default unless a shot below says otherwise.
Landscape. Full body in frame with room above your head and below your feet.

THE FIXED SEQUENCE - always this order, no warm-up reps inside the take
1. Split stance hold, side view - 10s each side
2. Low stride walk, side view - 4 strides
3. 3-step drive to 1-step stop, side view - 2 each side
4. Zig-zag drive stride, front view - 2 each way
5. Closeout to contain 3 slides, baseline view - 2 each side
6. Single-leg landing from an angle, front view - 2 each side
7. Passing Block A drive and kick, baseline view - 2 each side

WHAT GETS READ FROM IT
Position: hip height at plant, shin angle, whether the front heel stays down, chest angle.
Dissociation: degrees of separation between shoulders and hips through the cut.
Deceleration: frames from full speed to motionless, and whether the stop takes one step or three.
Symmetry: every one of those measured left against right.
Passing: eye direction, drift out of the jump stop, live dribble versus extra gather.

WHAT TO KEEP
One folder per month, named by date. Never delete an old one - the old ones ARE the baseline.
Write the date here so the month is findable later:
LAST FILMED: ___

NOT BUILT YET
Automatic joint-angle and timing analysis is a separate build and does not exist right now.
Film it anyway. The footage is the asset, and the analysis can be run over it later - including
retroactively across every month you have already captured. Months you skip are gone forever.
